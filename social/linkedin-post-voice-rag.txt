--- POST ---

An LLM will happily invent a customer care number that doesn't exist.

Ask a plain model "what is the customer care number?" and it either refuses or makes one up. That's not a small bug — for an enterprise assistant, a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer.

So I built a small voice agent to show the fix, in the hardest setting I could pick: mixed Kannada + English, spoken.

The setup:

→ Speech-to-text: Sarvam Saaras v3, in "codemix" mode — because generic models (Whisper, and to a degree Amazon Transcribe) garble code-mixed Kannada-English.

→ LLM + embeddings: AWS Bedrock — Claude for reasoning, Titan for embeddings.

→ RAG: retrieve the right document, then force the model to answer only from it, and cite the source.

→ Speech back out in Kannada.

What the demo shows, same model, same question:

Without RAG - "I don't have that information."
With RAG - the exact toll-free number, the hours, and a source citation.

The honest part most RAG demos skip: on public facts the model already knows (what a common appliance error code means), RAG adds little. Its real value shows up on private, current, or specific data — your helpline, your warranty, this week's pricing. There the model has to guess, and RAG replaces the guess with a grounded, cited fact.

The win isn't a bigger model. It's retrieval plus traceability.

The whole thing runs on free tiers and a few cents of Bedrock, from one Jupyter notebook — code, architecture diagrams, and video in the write-up.

If you're building voice or RAG for Indian languages, or want your team to learn to build this properly, the full walkthrough (and how to work with me) is in the comments. 👇
